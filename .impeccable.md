# Movie Night design context

## Target audience

Individuals, couples, families, and small groups who want to watch a movie but
feel overwhelmed by large streaming catalogues. They are likely choosing on a
sofa, often in a dim room, on a phone, tablet, or television-adjacent laptop.

## Core use case

Move from a small set of meaningful preferences to one confident movie
recommendation quickly. If the recommendation misses, let the user ask for one
different choice without dropping them back into an endless catalogue.

## Brand personality

Decisive, theatrical, and tactile. The interface should recall entering a
beloved independent cinema: dark auditorium walls, oxblood velvet, warm brass,
cream lightbox panels, condensed changeable-letter typography, and the moment
the house lights fall before a film begins.

The experience is nostalgic but not kitsch, premium but not exclusive, and
playful without becoming a novelty interface.

## Design direction

- Concept: **House Lights Down**.
- Input phase: a luminous “Now Showing” programme board that progressively
  narrows the evening's programme.
- Reveal phase: a wide cinema screen becomes the stage for exactly one featured
  recommendation.
- Memorable element: the interface physically changes state from foyer light to
  auditorium darkness as the recommendation is prepared and revealed.
- Default theme: dark, derived from likely evening viewing and the cinema-room
  metaphor; high-luminance panels are reserved for focused decision surfaces.

## Constraints

- Mobile-first and usable one-handed where practical.
- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and interaction requirements.
- Respect reduced-motion, reduced-transparency, and high-contrast preferences.
- Never rely on colour, animation, or decorative typography alone to convey
  state.
- Avoid an endless poster grid; the product should reduce choices.
- Keep the system framework-independent until the application stack is chosen.
